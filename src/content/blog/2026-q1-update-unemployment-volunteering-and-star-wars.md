## Unemployment

I entered my Unemployment Era at the end of January when Amazon abruptly laid me off. In their official [press release](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-layoffs-corporate-jan-2026), Amazon cited a need to reduce bureaucracy, which is fine and all. Because ultimately, I do not care why they did it, nor do I harbor any hard feelings towards coworkers and managers who survived the corporate culling this time. During my time there, I tried my best and worked with a lot of cool, smart, and funny people. And thus, I carry forward no regrets.

However, the job market for software engineers is quite shit right now. Between the push for AI and America’s self-induced economic turmoil, it’s hard for me to picture what the industry will look like, even six months from now. I’m taking it slow with finding a new job, which is not to say that I haven’t been interviewing at all, but I am rather unenthused about the whole thing. As of writing this, I have already failed a technical screen with Tiktok, to give you a picture of how it’s going.

![](/images/blog/pxl_20260315_164222149~3.jpg)

Without the stress and structure of corporate work, I have quickly retreated to a life of hedonism. I wake up naturally around 8 AM without any sense of urgency on most days. With my phone in hand, I laze in bed for 30 minutes until I feel like getting up for breakfast. I make plans with friends and romp around NYC in search of shiny new experiences. On days I do not have plans, I take naps in the afternoon as I please.

With the exception of the various human tasks I am burdened with like filing taxes and paying mortgage, I imagine this is how a fulfilled house pet feels.

## Volunteering

As with all great things in life, hedonism also must be enjoyed in moderation. And I have come into the widely-accepted and uncontested idea that too much phone time is not only bad for you, but actively detrimental to your development as a person. So to fill some of my newfound time, I sought out an activity that I frequently thought about doing, but was largely unavailable to those working 9-to-5’s: volunteering.

![Putting a frozen chicken into bags to be passed out to the community](/images/blog/volunteering.jpg)

This is a list of organizations I have done volunteer work with over the past two months:

* [New York Cares](https://www.newyorkcares.org/): This organization connects volunteers with local non-profits/organizations looking for volunteer help. I’ve found most of my volunteer work through their portal. I’ve helped out with the New York Cares coat drive, distributed food at a Salvation Army food pantry, and helped review resumes from job seekers. It’s a good place to start for people who want to dabble in a wide variety of experiences.
* [Bowery Mission](https://www.bowery.org/volunteer/): I volunteered at their Bowery Campus cafeteria doing meal prep between lunch and dinner. The first time I was there, they had me laying out bacon on trays for an hour. The second time, they had me grating massive hunks of American cheese and prepping trays for dinner. I prefer the diverse activities that come with meal prep, but you can also sign up to help serve breakfast, lunch, and dinner.

This kind of volunteering is an easy way to experience a wide breadth of new things with minimal risk and commitment. It’s also a great way to maintain a semblance of self-worth and gives you a low-cost and meaningful reason to leave your house in the sudden absence of corporate structure. I’m not going to get on my soapbox about it, but I would highly recommend it to people who have the time and want to engage in their local community.

## Star Wars

I have completed my watch of the original trilogy (Episodes 4-6)!

![](/images/blog/starwars.jpg)

The first Star Wars movie I ever watched was Episode 8: The Last Jedi. With no prior knowledge of Star Wars, I saw it in theaters during its release with a friend who had a huge crush on Kylo Ren, and to my exact expectations, I did not understand a thing. A different friend recently told me that Episode 8 was a terrible movie to start with, and that it was so shit that it completely obliterated his interest in the franchise.

And then in 2019, without having made any effort to fill the gaps in my Star Wars knowledge since Episode 8, I was back at the local AMC for Episode 9: The Rise of Skywalker.

I was pleasantly surprised to find that Episode 4 was actually kind of good and that starting a story from the beginning really allows you to understand what is happening. As a work of science fiction, it is quite optimistic about the possible relationship that humans could have with technology one day, an ironic contrast to the current predicament we live in.

Though I had learned of Darth Vader’s iconic “I am your father” line via osmosis through the membranes of pop culture, I did not recall or know that Luke and Leia were siblings. Halfway through episode 5, I reported to my friends that whatever Luke and Leia had going on was an incredible nothingburger to me, and that Han Solo and Leia undeniably had more chemistry despite being incredibly annoying towards and around each other.
