## Prologue: Osaka Station

The train ride from Kyoto Station to Osaka Station is a mere **30-minute ride** on the special rapid Tokaido-Sanyo Line, which is less time than it took for me to get to my office in midtown every day for work. The American mind cannot fathom two major cities so close to each other!

If you plan on making this journey as a traveler, do it between 12pm-3pm, safely outside of peak commuting hours to reduce the shame and inconvenience of bringing entire checked-sized bags on the train.

![](/images/blog/travel_isogu.webp)

This brings me to the purpose of including this section: If you are traveling with checked-size luggage and need to transfer at Osaka Station, **it is dastardly difficult to locate elevators** that definitely exist according to the official Osaka Metro website.

![](/images/blog/osaka-station-map.webp)

(For the curious, we were transferring from the Tokaido-Sanyo Line to the Yotsubashi Line - Nishi Umeda Station)

Osaka Station is a massive sprawling station that provides access to many metro lines, yet elevators are frequently not marked on signage. Whenever we came to a flight of stairs, it was not always obvious where the corresponding elevator would be, and sometimes the elevator would be pretty far from the stairs.

![Google Maps screenshot of the Osaka Station Area showing the spread of the station](/images/blog/osaka-station.webp "It's kind of like the Times Square station in NYC")

Look at the spread of this station!!

My friends and I eventually gave up looking for elevators in some places and took the stairs with our luggage instead. Some people do not have the ability to do this, so Osaka Station should really look into fixing their signage!!

## Accommodation

We stayed at [Hotel Cordia Osaka](https://cordia-osaka.com/en/), a 3-star hotel located centrally in the city.

![](/images/blog/hotel-cordia.webp)

The room we stayed in was billed as a Hollywood Twin, with 2 twin beds and a sofa bed, but as it turns out, the sofa bed was basically just a normal bed.

Hotel Cordia had the most comfortable mattresses of all the hotels I stayed at in Japan this trip, and the cost per night was pretty affordable. If you’re looking for a place to stay in Osaka, Hotel Cordia is fine. (Note that they also have a location in Hommachi called Hotel Cordia Osaka Hommachi. I’m sure they’re also fine, but I didn’t stay at that one.)

## Notable Excursions

### Teppanyaki (Teppanyaki Restaurant TAIEN)

About an hour after our Osaka Station Experience, we went to [Teppanyaki restaurant TAIEN](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Um4eaEfUg1kdWReZ7), a short walk from our hotel in the basement of an unassuming building. This is a mid-to-higher end Teppanyaki place, with the dinner courses starting at 9000yen (~$60 USD).![](IMAGE_PLACEHOLDER)
Course appetizer
It was a random Tuesday night so the staff member looked a little
confused when four young foreigners without a reservation showed up. She
showed us the course menu and prices to make sure we were okay with it,
and then got us seated.

Teppanyaki is a style of cooking where the chef cooks this meal right in front of you on a large flat iron griddle. In the US, this is known as Hibachi. Hibachi typically refers to a smaller charcoal grill, but the terms Teppanyaki and Hibachi have become [interchangeable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teppanyaki#In_the_United_States) in the US. The reasons for this are debated in this Reddit [thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/JapaneseFood/comments/1j6pb6u/why_do_americans_call_teppanyaki_hibachi/).

Anyway, **picture a steakhouse hibachi**. I embarrassed myself by not knowing the difference between fillet and sirloin, and then again by not knowing how to respond to “How would you like your steak cooked?”

![](/images/blog/teppanyaki-2.webp "Main Course")

The food was delicious and beautifully presented. (There was also a salad and a soup between the appetizer and the main course, but I didn't take pictures of it)

![](/images/blog/houjicha-ice-cream.webp "Houjicha ice cream for dessert")

The staff member serving us and cleaning the grill was super friendly even though her English wasn’t the best, and our Japanese was worse. She asked about our trip and where we had been. She probably also gave us recommendations, but I honestly can’t remember much of the conversation...

### Kinji Used Clothing

A lot of thrift/vintage stores in Japan primarily focus on carrying used designer brand items, which isn’t really my scene. I learned from this [Reddit post](https://www.reddit.com/r/Osaka/comments/1iwnkjd/cheap_used_clothingthrift_stores_in_osaka_not) that for actual cheap secondhand clothing, you need to be looking at a category of stores called furugiya (古着屋). The big ones are: 2nd Street, TreFacStyle, and Kinji. You can find them all over Japan.

![](/images/blog/shopping_sale.webp)

Side note: Having been to a lot of 2nd streets in Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka, I can vouch for 2nd Street as a good place to start if you’re looking for cheap clothing. They always have more expensive stuff up front, but if you walk into the back, you can find a lot of clothing for under 1000yen, which is 6 USD. My friend scored three pairs of pants for under 20 USD.

In Osaka, we went to the Kinji in the Big Step Mall by America-mura, which had a wide selection of items. The store was loosely organized by style (80s, 90s, Y2K) in addition to the usual categories (gender, shirt, pants, etc).

![](/images/blog/kinji.webp)

I had a lot of fun browsing this store. I bought a pretty sick coat here for about 25 USD, so I wanted to give it a shout out.

![](/images/blog/haul.webp)

### Tavern Pachimon Wars

This [izakaya](https://maps.app.goo.gl/MsFL1q6EMCYS1F7g6) is for the Star Wars lovers. The Japanese word Pachimon (パチモン) means counterfeit, and they proudly brand themselves as the “[Bootleg SW Toy Bar](https://www.osaka.com/wards/pachimon-wars-osakas-bootleg-star-wars-bar/).” If I had to guess, they probably avoid the full name “star wars” in any of their own branding to avoid copyright issues.

![](/images/blog/starwarssign.webp)

They’re decked from head to toe in an impressive amount of bootleg Star Wars merch. The owner, who has been running the bar for 8 years, has watched every show and movie that has come out.

When my friends and I entered the bar, the TVs were playing Solo with Japanese subs. Think of a sports bar, but they just watch Star Wars the entire time.

![](/images/blog/pachimon-interior.webp)

![](/images/blog/han-solo.webp)

The drinks menu was delightfully thematic, the food was delicious, and the bathroom was hilarious.

![](/images/blog/drink-menu.webp)

![](/images/blog/pachimon-bathroom.webp)

I had the Yoda (Yogurt Liqueur + Soda), and it was great.

![](/images/blog/pachimon-drink.webp)

Although the owner isn’t entirely fluent in English, he will try to connect with you over a mutual love of star wars by telling you about various toys in his store and showing you pictures of cosplay he has done. His passion transcends the language barrier. He also has this English-language booklet that provides commentary on the various bootleg toys in his bar.

![](/images/blog/pachimon-guide.webp)

He mentions in the booklet that he only collects bootleg toys that make him laugh, which I think is a great principle.

![](/images/blog/pachimon-guide-1.webp)

He said that Star Wars isn’t super popular in Japan because Japan has a lot of domestic IP that competes with it, so a lot of his visitors are foreigners like us. If you are a Star Wars fan, please consider visiting!

## Other Mentions

* I walked through Dotonbori and saw the big Glico man. It was alright. It probably would’ve been nicer if it hadn’t been raining.
* The women’s restroom in Big Step Mall was kind of crazy.

![](/images/blog/big-step-mall-bathroom.webp)

* The men’s restroom in Big Step Mall was even crazier. From the entrance corridors, the entire bathroom is lined in mirrors, including the walls of each stall. This meant that you could watch yourself pee from three different angles. Hearing this from my friend, I lamented the fact that I wasn't a man because I would've loved to see it.

![](/images/blog/big-step-mall-other-bathroom.webp)

![](IMAGE\_PLACEHOLDER "Imagine being able to see yourself pee from multiple angles....")

* The owner of Tavern Pachimon Wars recommended us a Hamburg steak restaurant near Osaka Castle, but it was closed so I never got to find out if it was good or not. Instead, my friends and I went to Okonomiyaki Chigusa, an okonomiyaki restaurant run by a Japanese grandmother who cutely refers to herself as obaachan. We spoke to her in Japanese, and she asked us if we were from Vietnam. We are not. The okonomiyaki was delicious.

![](/images/blog/okonomiyaki.webp)

* In Tokyo and Kyoto, people stood on the left side of the escalators and passed on the right. For some reason, it was the opposite in Osaka, and people stood on the right, much like in the US. However, there have been local campaigns in Japan to get people to stop walking on escalators and treat both sides as standing.
* Overall, the vibe in Osaka was more relaxed than Tokyo and Kyoto. The staff at restaurants actually tried to make small talk with us in Osaka. In Tokyo and Kyoto, it felt that staff only interacted with us as far as necessary to get their job done.
